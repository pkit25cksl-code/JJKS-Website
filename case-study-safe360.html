Case study

Interactive 360° health & safety training

Turning passive classroom safety training into something warehouse staff actively do, and remember.

Client
Logistics & warehousing (automotive)
Service
Requirements, prototyping, web
Timeline
August – October 2026
Team
4 people, agile sprints

The challenge

Our client runs a logistics and warehousing business serving several automotive companies, and has to meet the safety standards those customers expect. Their health and safety training relied on presentations, printed material and classroom sessions.

That approach had two problems. Trainees found it passive, so attention drifted. And there was no reliable way to show that the knowledge had actually been absorbed, which matters in a warehouse where a missed hazard can mean a serious injury.

Our approach

We started by listening. In our first meeting we played the brief back to the client to check our understanding, then gathered requirements around the people who would use the system: warehouse staff taking the training, and the trainers who set it up and check results.

We wrote everything into a Software Requirements Specification, which the client reviewed and confirmed before the build began. When the client asked for virtual reality headsets to be optional rather than required, we updated the specification so the platform would work in an ordinary web browser first.

What the platform does

The agreed specification set out a training experience built around doing rather than watching:

  • 360° training scenes that trainees explore with a mouse, touch screen or keyboard.
  • Hazard spotting and decisions, with challenges and puzzles embedded in each scene.
  • Quizzes with instant feedback, explaining why an answer is right or wrong, and a retry if the pass mark isn't met.
  • One-vs-one challenges and high scores to encourage people to come back.
  • Progress tracking and reports so trainers can see who has completed what.

What we learned

  • Play the brief back early. Saying our understanding out loud in the first meeting surfaced corrections before they cost anything.
  • Depth beats breadth. With a small team and a fixed timeline, doing one module really well is more useful than six shallow ones.
  • The client owns the content. We build the experience; the client's safety experts check every piece of safety content for accuracy.
  • Browser first. Making VR optional meant staff could train on equipment the client already has.
