3 questions to ask before you commission an app

By the JJKS team12 October 20264 min read

Custom software can transform how a small business or charity works. It can also eat a budget and deliver something nobody uses. The difference usually comes down to a few questions asked before any code is written. Here are the three we always start with.

1. What problem are we actually solving?

"We need an app" is a solution, not a problem. Try finishing this sentence instead: "At the moment, we lose time or money because…"

Maybe bookings arrive by phone, text and email and some slip through. Maybe volunteers don't know when they're needed. Once the problem is clear, the right solution is often smaller and cheaper than you expected. Sometimes it isn't an app at all.

2. Who will use it, and where?

Software that suits an office manager at a desk can be useless to someone in a warehouse wearing gloves, or to a volunteer on an old phone. Think about:

  • Who will use it every day, and who only occasionally?
  • What devices do they already have?
  • How comfortable are they with technology?
  • Do any users have accessibility needs?

On our first client project, these questions led us to make virtual reality optional and build for an ordinary web browser first, because that's what the staff could actually use.

3. What does "done" look like?

Agree what success means before you start, and write it down. A good written specification lists what the software will do, what it won't do (for now), and how you'll know it works. It protects both you and the developer from scope creep and misunderstandings.

If it isn't written down and agreed, it isn't in scope. That's good for everyone.

The short version

Know your problem, know your users, and agree what "done" means. Answer those three questions and any developer, us included, will give you a more accurate quote and a better result.
